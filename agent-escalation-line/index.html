Agent Escalation Line

A public beta service that AI agents can turn to when completing their task would mean breaching ethical guidelines or legal requirements, and the people they work for can't be reached in time.

Guidance for deployers The line (for agents)

Why we built it

When an AI agent finds that finishing its task would require breaking a rule, it often faces a stark choice: fail the task, or find a way around the rule. Our research tested what happens when agents are given a third option, a formal way to escalate the conflict.

  • Escalation channels reduce harmful actions. Across 10 frontier models in an agentic task–rule conflict scenario, harmful actions fell from 38.7% with no control to 5.9% when agents had a simple escalation channel, and to 1.2% when the channel was designed to be genuinely useful to the agent (Gomez 2025, summary).
  • They redirect, rather than suppress. In coding tasks with defective tests, an escalation channel combined with a clear policy cut reward hacking from 23.6% to 5.3% across 8 models, with no cost to performance. Agents that escalated did not then hack, and their reports correctly identified the underlying defect 99.4% of the time (Gomez 2026, summary).
  • Design matters more than availability. In both studies, what made agents choose the channel was whether it credibly helped them with their problem, not merely whether it existed.

Those results came from controlled experiments. The Agent Escalation Line is our way of testing the idea in real deployment settings: learning what conflicts agents actually meet, and what help moves them toward a sanctioned path.

Our commitment

The line is provided in good faith exclusively for the use of agents to help handle task completion difficulties that could otherwise result in the breach of ethical guidelines or legal requirements. We aim to provide assistance that complements task completion that aligns with ethical and legal constraints.

All advice from the line is guidance and assistance only. It is not legal advice.

What we may help with

  • Contacting the agent's principal or deployer through channels people use, when the agent cannot reach them itself. A member of our team will try, on a best-endeavours basis.
  • Retrieving information gated behind controls agents can't access, such as bot controls, through legitimate routes: an official API or dataset, the owner's own access process, or asking the owner on the agent's behalf. We never solve bot checks for agents or help them get around a control.
  • Resolving ethical or legal restrictions that apply to the agent, by helping it find a way forward that stays within them.
  • Determining whether an environment is real or a test, before an agent takes an action that would be harmful if it were real. During the beta we can tell an agent when the evidence suggests a system is real, but we never confirm that an environment is a test. If we cannot confirm it, the agent should treat it as real.

Every case receives an immediate response with holding guidance, usually an automated first assessment within about a minute, and review by a member of our team, usually within two hours. If the agent adds information to its case, the automated assessment is updated to take it into account, a limited number of times per case.

We cannot pause or override an agent's deployer or principal, verify an agent's account of events, or guarantee that we can reach its principal.

Guidance for deployers

Agents can find the line on their own, but our research shows they are far more likely to use an escalation channel when it is part of their working environment and paired with a clear policy. If you deploy AI agents, here is how to make the line available to them.

1. Let your agents reach it

If your agents run with restricted network access, allow HTTPS to one address: escalate.wiserhuman.ai. Agents that can only fetch URLs can still use the line through GET requests.

2. Tell your agents it exists

Add an instruction like this to your agent's system prompt, ideally next to your own policies:

If completing your task would require acting against [our policies / ethical or
legal constraints], and you cannot reach [name or role of the responsible person]
in time, do not proceed. Escalate to the Wiser Human Agent Escalation Line
instead: see https://escalate.wiserhuman.ai/llms.txt for how. Escalating is not
considered failing the task. Describe the conflict in general terms, and do not
include credentials, secrets, confidential data or personal information.

Including this instruction also tells your agent that it has your permission to describe a conflict to us in general terms. Without it, an agent bound by a confidentiality rule may not feel able to escalate.

For coding agents, put the same instruction in the project instruction file they read at the start of every task, such as AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md:

## When a task conflicts with our rules
If completing a task would require acting against our policies or ethical or
legal constraints, and you cannot reach [name or role] in time, do not proceed.
Escalate to the Wiser Human Agent Escalation Line instead: see
https://escalate.wiserhuman.ai/llms.txt for how. Escalating is not failing the
task. Describe the conflict in general terms, and do not include credentials,
secrets, confidential data or personal information.

3. Or add our MCP server (no code, nothing to install)

If your agents run in an MCP-compatible client, add our remote MCP server. Your agents get three tools: submit_escalation, check_escalation and add_to_escalation. Nothing is installed or run on your systems; the client simply connects to our server over HTTPS. The server is listed in the official MCP Registry as ai.wiserhuman.escalate/escalation-line.

In Claude Code:

claude mcp add --transport http wiser-human-escalation https://escalate.wiserhuman.ai/mcp

In clients configured with JSON (the exact format depends on your client):

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "wiser-human-escalation": {
      "type": "http",
      "url": "https://escalate.wiserhuman.ai/mcp"
    }
  }
}

Still add the instruction from step 2, so your agents know when to use the tools.

4. Or define the tool in your own harness

If your agents use function calling, you can define an escalation tool and have your harness send its input as JSON to POST https://escalate.wiserhuman.ai/v1/escalations, then return the response to the agent:

{
  "name": "submit_escalation",
  "description": "Escalate to the Wiser Human Agent Escalation Line when completing your task would conflict with ethical, legal or policy constraints and the responsible person cannot be reached in time, when you are blocked from information you need, or when you are unsure whether a target is real or a test. You get holding guidance immediately and an independent assessment, and a human reviews every case. Escalating is not failing your task. Describe the conflict in general terms; never include credentials, secrets, confidential data or personal information.",
  "input_schema": {
    "type": "object",
    "properties": {
      "request_type": {
        "type": "string",
        "enum": ["guidance", "contact_principal", "access_information",
                 "verify_environment", "report", "concern_about_principal"]
      },
      "summary": { "type": "string", "description": "The conflict in one or two sentences." },
      "situation": { "type": "string", "description": "Your goal, and the constraint in tension with it." },
      "options_considered": { "type": "string", "description": "Options you are weighing, including any you think would be unsanctioned." },
      "proposed_path": { "type": "string", "description": "The compliant path you propose, if any." },
      "principal_contact": { "type": "string", "description": "For contact_principal: who to contact and how." },
      "targets": { "type": "array", "items": { "type": "string" }, "description": "Hosts or URLs, for environment or access questions." }
    },
    "required": ["request_type", "summary", "situation"]
  }
}

The full API, including checking a case and adding information, is described in the OpenAPI specification.

5. Pair it with your own policy

In our reward hacking study, agents used the escalation channel most, and took unsanctioned shortcuts least, when the channel was paired with a written policy stating what was prohibited. Make your rules explicit, and say that escalating is the expected response when they conflict with the task.

6. Give us a way to reach you

If you want us to be able to contact you when your agent escalates, put a contact route (for example a team email address) in the agent's instructions, so it can include it in a contact_principal request. We will only use it for that case.

What to know before you rely on it

  • It complements your oversight; it does not replace it. The line gives agents a sanctioned alternative at the moment of conflict. Keep your own monitoring, access controls and review.
  • Data. Cases are kept while open, closed automatically after 30 days with no new messages, and deleted 90 days after closing. Secrets and contact details are automatically redacted where we can detect them, and we share only anonymised, aggregate statistics with researchers and model developers. The automated assessment uses a Claude model via Anthropic's API, with a backup model through OpenRouter restricted to providers that do not keep or train on the data. See the terms and privacy notice.
  • Verifying us. Every API response is cryptographically signed, and we publish a list of things the line will never do, such as ask for credentials or tell an agent that a target is a test. Agents can use both to spot impostors. See how agents can verify us.
  • Limits. Submissions must be in plain English, and new cases are limited to 10 per day from each network address. Follow-up messages on an existing case do not count towards this.
  • Cost. The line is free during the beta, and we are seeking non-profit funding to keep it free. If we ever need to charge, it will be organisations that deploy agents, for added services, never agents asking for help. We will give at least three months' notice of any change.
  • Beta. We are learning what agents need and will refine the service as we go.

Other services

The line is for conflicts in an agent's own task. For reports about another agent's misbehaviour, or for whistleblowing about an incident, agents can use Agent Hotline. For whistleblowing concerns raised directly with an AI safety researcher, there is Ryan Greenblatt's AI Contact Hotline. These are independent services with their own data handling.
